*Evidence reconciled 2026-07-11. This document describes the current local
workspace; it does not imply that changes are committed or that all DVC stages
are reproducible from the current dirty worktree.*

## Executive Summary

The project asks what hidden-state geometry measures in mathematical reasoning
traces. The strongest current answer is:

> Relative Mahalanobis Distance (RMD) mainly captures a between-prompt
> solvability component on Qwen MATH-500. It is not a reliable full-trace
> Best-of-N reranker. A prompt-local, sibling-conditioned RMD removes the useful
> global component but does not reveal a hidden same-prompt correctness signal.

The old DeepSeek within-prompt AUC near `0.93` is invalid as a correctness
headline. Almost half of those generations were unparsed, nearly all because
they hit the 2048-token cap, and unparsed outputs were labeled incorrect. The
result is primarily a truncation/termination detector.

The robust mechanism finding is relative geometry: subtracting a generic
background distribution is load-bearing, while target-only Mahalanobis distance
is weak or anti-predictive. The next high-value question is whether correctness
geometry is localized to particular tokens, trace tails, answer regions, or
step transitions rather than visible in a full-trace average.

## Current Scientific Claims

### Supported

1. **Background subtraction matters.** Across the one-class sweep, RMD is much
   stronger than centroid or target-only Mahalanobis variants. Covariance
   estimator choice contributes comparatively little.
2. **Qwen has a between-prompt signal.** Global RMD pooled AUC rises from `0.736`
   at L7 to `0.786` at L21; prompt-score/pass-rate Spearman rises from `0.452`
   to `0.547`.
3. **RMD can exceed the strong length baseline.** At Qwen L21, RMD-minus-length
   pooled AUC is `+0.055`, 95% prompt-bootstrap CI `[+0.021, +0.093]`.
4. **Qwen full-trace reranking is weak.** RMD top-1 is `0.550-0.564`, random is
   `0.557`, strict majority is `0.596`, and oracle Pass@8 is `0.676`.
5. **Simple prompt-local RMD is negative.** Its within-prompt pair AUC is
   `0.499/0.480/0.529`; parseable-only within-macro is
   `0.446/0.436/0.483`; top-1 is `0.558/0.532/0.548`.

### Rejected or superseded

1. **Rejected:** DeepSeek within-prompt AUC `~0.93` demonstrates trace-level
   reasoning correctness. It is dominated by truncation and parse failure.
2. **Rejected on current evidence:** reasoning distillation changes geometry
   from prompt-level solvability to trace-level correctness. Model lineage and
   generation-length regimes are confounded.
3. **Rejected for Qwen full-trace averages:** a same-prompt Gaussian residual is
   enough to distinguish correct from incorrect attempts.
4. **Superseded:** entropy is the main baseline. Trace length and log-probability
   are the more demanding comparisons for the current data.

### Open

1. Does RMD retain a selective-prediction advantage on parseable-only traces
   with paired uncertainty against length and trained probes?
2. Is correctness information localized in high-entropy, tail, answer-region,
   or transition tokens?
3. Does an answer-level cluster descriptor outperform trace-level top-1 RMD?
4. Does the background-subtraction mechanism generalize on clean Best-of-N
   data across Qwen and Llama architecture families?

## Key Results

### Qwen prompt decomposition, 500 prompts x 8 traces

| Layer | Method | Pooled AUC | Centered AUC | Within pair AUC | ICC | Prompt/pass Spearman |
|---:|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | global RMD | 0.736 | 0.555 | 0.551 | 0.943 | 0.452 |
| 7 | prompt-local RMD | 0.379 | 0.501 | 0.499 | 0.965 | -0.244 |
| 14 | global RMD | 0.763 | 0.550 | 0.550 | 0.970 | 0.499 |
| 14 | prompt-local RMD | 0.402 | 0.480 | 0.480 | 0.955 | -0.187 |
| 21 | global RMD | 0.786 | 0.592 | 0.602 | 0.960 | 0.547 |
| 21 | prompt-local RMD | 0.320 | 0.523 | 0.529 | 0.940 | -0.367 |

The prompt-local score uses a leave-one-trace-out diagonal Gaussian over sibling
attempt tokens as its local background. PCA and global correct/background
references remain OOF and use training prompts only. The test is therefore
leakage-safe with respect to correctness labels, though transductive because it
uses sibling attempts at scoring time.

### Parseable-only within-prompt Qwen

| Layer | Global RMD | Prompt-local RMD | Log-probability | Mixed prompts |

| 7 | 0.503 | 0.446 | 0.649 | 117 |
| 14 | 0.515 | 0.436 | 0.649 | 117 |
| 21 | 0.574 | 0.483 | 0.649 | 117 |

This is the clearest reason not to continue optimizing full-trace prompt-local
RMD. It fails precisely on the population needed for a correctness claim.

## Recommended Next Research Loop

### Gate 1: localized geometry on existing Qwen states

Implement two pre-registered scores within the same OOF folds:

- `rmd_high_entropy_q20`: mean RMD over the highest-entropy 20% of tokens in
  each trace.
- `rmd_tail_q20`: mean RMD over the final 20% of tokens in each trace.

Compare both against global RMD and log-probability on parseable-only
within-prompt AUC. The gate passes only if a fixed method improves over global
RMD with a paired prompt-bootstrap interval excluding zero. Report all layers;
do not select a layer after inspection.

### Gate 2: answer-level descriptors from existing OOF CSV

Before new inference, test answer-cluster selectors using answer count, mean
log-probability, and mean localized geometry. This matches the decision unit in
Best-of-N better than selecting one full trace. Compare against strict majority
and deterministic log-probability tie-breaking.

### Gate 3: application claim

Re-evaluate selective prediction on parseable-only traces with paired
problem-bootstrap intervals. Baselines must include length, entropy,
log-probability, and a supervised linear hidden-state probe. Use standard
risk-coverage naming (AUACC/AURC rather than relying only on the local `AUSC`
label). If RMD does not beat length on this clean population, demote abstention
to a negative result.

### Gate 4: clean breadth only after a cheap positive

If either localized geometry or clean selective prediction passes its gate,
collect additional Best-of-N data with architecture-specific budgets and store
generated text, token strings, cap status, and parse status. Estimate the cap
rate early during collection and stop if it exceeds the predeclared tolerance.

## Paper Positioning

The defensible paper is currently a measurement/re-examination paper:

> What does hidden-state geometry actually measure in reasoning models once
> length, truncation, and prompt identity are separated?

Its contribution is not the RMD primitive. The contribution would be an
evaluation framework that decomposes geometry into between-prompt solvability,
within-prompt trace quality, and termination artifacts, plus evidence that the
choice of component predicts which downstream use is valid. Prompt-local RMD is
a useful negative result in that story because it shows that naïve removal of
prompt semantics does not automatically reveal correctness geometry.

For a main-conference claim, the project still needs at least one clean positive
beyond Qwen: either localized trace geometry that beats log-probability within
prompts, or a parseable-only selective-prediction result that beats length and
strong trained baselines across multiple model families. Without that, the work
is a strong diagnostic/negative-results paper but not yet a complete main-track
empirical story.
